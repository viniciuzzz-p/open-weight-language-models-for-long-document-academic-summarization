## Overview

This repository contains the dataset, analysis scripts, and replication materials for our submitted paper. This work evaluates 19 open-weight language models on the automatic generation of abstracts from full academic theses. The models were executed locally with extended context windows, utilizing the Chain of Density (CoD) iterative prompting strategy designed to maximize information density. The evaluation employed a custom Abstract Quality (AQ) metric based on the LLM-as-a-Judge paradigm, utilizing GPT-5 as the evaluator.

The study includes comparisons against a strong proprietary reference (Gemini 2.5 Pro) and a simple extractive baseline (Lead-N), alongside an ablation study to measure the specific impact of the CoD strategy against a direct zero-shot approach. Results indicate better performance in larger-scale models, highlighting the *gpt-oss-120b*, while also evidencing the high competitiveness of intermediate architectures such as *qwen3-30b-a3b*.

## Dataset Description

The evaluation benchmark was constructed from a sample of 30 PhD theses extracted from the ORA Thesis Collection (Oxford University Research Archive). 

To ensure domain diversity, the sampling covered four major academic divisions:
* Mathematical, Physical and Life Sciences (MPLS)
* Medical Sciences (MSD)
* Social Sciences (SSD)
* Humanities (HUMS)

**Mitigation of Data Leakage:** To ensure the integrity of the zero-shot evaluation and prevent data contamination, all selected documents were published and made publicly available online between **November 2025 and May 2026**. This timeframe is strictly subsequent to the knowledge cutoff dates of the evaluated open-weight models.

### Pre-processing and Noise Robustness

The original abstracts were formally isolated from the body text provided as input to the models and were reserved exclusively to act as the ground truth in the automated evaluation stage. Footnotes and figure captions were deliberately preserved in the input text to act as a noise robustness challenge. The dataset acts as a stress-test for context windows, with text loads reaching peaks of up to 126,018 tokens.

## Prompting and Evaluation Protocol

* **Generation Phase:** The primary generation utilized a standardized English prompt structured upon the **Chain of Density (CoD)** technique. Models were instructed to produce abstracts across 5 iterations, progressively increasing semantic density without expanding the word count.
* **Baselines and Ablation:** To establish comparative thresholds, the study incorporates **Lead-N** (extracting the initial section of the thesis) and a **Zero-Shot Ablation** applied to representative models (4B, 30B, and 120B) to isolate the specific impact of the CoD approach.
* **Validation Phase:** Generated abstracts were evaluated via the LLM-as-a-Judge methodology using the **DeepEval** framework powered by **GPT-5**. We defined a custom generative metric named Abstract Quality (AQ).
* **Evaluation Dimensions:** The judge evaluated five strict dimensions: preservation of central scientific elements, semantic faithfulness, critical omissions detection, stylistic adequacy, and conciseness.

## System Requirements and Local Inference Infrastructure

All inferences were executed locally (on-premise) to investigate technical feasibility and extended context window sustainability. The experimental setup relied on the following hardware and software stack:

### Data Analysis and Pre-processing

* **Environment:** Python 3.x
* **Key Libraries:** `pandas` for dataset manipulation, and `matplotlib`/`seaborn` for generating standardized academic figures.
* **Notebooks:** Jupyter environment utilized for exploratory data analysis, descriptive statistics, and tokenization metrics calculation.

### Software and Backend

* **Inference Engine:** Inferences were executed using the LM Studio server environment utilizing the `llama.cpp` backend.
* **Model Format:** All 19 evaluated models operated entirely in the GGUF file format.
* **Execution Parameters:** The models were standardized to extended context windows. The generation limit was set to 8,192 tokens with a temperature of 0.3.
* **Quantization Schemes:** To enable the execution of high-scale models on local hardware, 4-bit compression schemes were employed. The DeepSeek, Gemma, LLaMA, Qwen, Phi, and Mistral families utilized traditional *Q4_K_M* quantization, while the *gpt-oss* models employed the *MXFP4* (Microscaling Formats 4-bit) standard.

### Hardware Specifications

The hardware infrastructure consisted of a workstation equipped with three distinct high-capacity GPUs to handle the VRAM requirements:
* 1x NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB VRAM)
* 2x NVIDIA RTX 5090 (64 GB total VRAM)
* 1x NVIDIA RTX A6000 (48 GB VRAM)

All models were loaded entirely into the video memory (VRAM) to maximize throughput and guarantee response time consistency.
